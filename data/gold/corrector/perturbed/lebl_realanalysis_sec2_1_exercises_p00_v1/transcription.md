### 2.1.4 Exercises

In the following exercises, feel free to use what you know from calculus to find the limit, if it exists. But you must prove that you found the correct limit, or prove that the sequence is divergent.

Exercise 2.1.1: Is the sequence $\{3n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ bounded? Prove or disprove.

Exercise 2.1.2: Is the sequence $\{n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.3: Is the sequence $\left\{\frac{(-1)^n}{2n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.4: Is the sequence $\{2^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.5: Is the sequence $\left\{\frac{n}{n+1}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.6: Is the sequence $\left\{\frac{n}{n^2+1}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.7: Let $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a sequence.

a) Show that $\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = 0$ (that is, the limit exists and is zero) if and only if $\lim_{n\to\infty} |x_n| = 0$.

b) Find an example such that $\{|x_n|\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ converges and $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ diverges.

Exercise 2.1.8: Is the sequence $\left\{\frac{2^n}{n!}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ convergent? If so, what is the limit?

Exercise 2.1.9: Show that the sequence $\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is monotone and bounded. Then use Theorem 2.1.10 to find the limit.

Exercise 2.1.10: Show that the sequence $\left\{\frac{n+1}{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is monotone and bounded. Then use Theorem 2.1.10 to find the limit.

Exercise 2.1.11: Finish the proof of Theorem 2.1.10 for monotone decreasing sequences.

Exercise 2.1.12: Prove Proposition 2.1.13.

Exercise 2.1.13: Let $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a convergent monotone sequence. Suppose there exists a $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = x_k.$$

Show that $x_n = x_k$ for all $n \ge k$.

Exercise 2.1.14: Find a convergent subsequence of the sequence $\{(-1)^n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$.

Exercise 2.1.15: Let $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a sequence defined by

$$x_n := \begin{cases} n & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}, \\ 1/n & \text{if } n \text{ is even}. \end{cases}$$

a) Is the sequence bounded? (prove or disprove)

b) Is there a convergent subsequence? If so, find it.
